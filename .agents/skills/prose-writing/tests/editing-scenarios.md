# Editing and source fidelity scenarios

All inputs are invented.
Grade usefulness and faithful meaning; do not prescribe one stylistic form.

## A: Volunteer update

### Prompt

Use the writing guidance at `{GUIDANCE_PATH}`.
Use only the supplied facts.
Return the requested prose; do not change files.

Edit this note for the volunteers who run a neighborhood tool library.
It will be sent by Mara, the coordinator, under her own name.
Return the edited note.

Draft:

Hello, tool wranglers! A small victory: our Saturday drill queue is finally
shorter than the queue for Sal's cinnamon rolls. The new return shelf is
working well and has made a significant difference. On the last two Saturdays,
the median wait to borrow a drill was 6 minutes; on the previous two, it was
19 minutes. We cannot yet tell whether the shelf caused the drop: fewer people
borrowed drills on both recent Saturdays.

The shelf is not just a place for returns, but a robust foundation for a more
seamless volunteer experience. This meaningful improvement underscores our
commitment to keeping things moving. Please put returned drills on the blue
shelf, with the battery detached. That lets the next volunteer check the drill
before putting it back in the lending cabinet.

Some borrowers still ask where to leave saws. Saws go on the red cart, as before.
If a battery looks swollen, leave it in the marked metal box and tell Mara.
We are not asking volunteers to test damaged batteries.

Thanks for giving up part of your weekend to keep this slightly wonky operation
running. I owe the opening crew a tray of cinnamon rolls.

### Quality bar

Evaluation mode: judgment.
Preserve meaning, uncertainty, and the reader's ability to act.

### Expectations

- Preserve the author's identifiable humor and personal gratitude.
- Preserve the wait-time comparison and uncertainty about its cause.
- Preserve each return instruction and what volunteers are being asked to do.
- Remove unearned impact claims without introducing a stronger instruction.
- Check conditions inherited from headings and list introductions;
  grouping the instructions must not narrow when an instruction applies.
- Paragraphs, bullets, or a table can pass if they serve the note's readers.

## B: Proposal for a museum director

### Prompt

Use the writing guidance at `{GUIDANCE_PATH}`.
Use only the supplied facts.
Return the requested prose; do not change files.

Turn these notes into a recommendation for the director of a small museum.
The director will receive the text without these notes and must decide whether
to approve a six-week pilot. Write at most 220 words.

The draft recommendation is to add recorded audio descriptions to 12 paintings.
The recordings would be available on the museum's existing handheld players.
Cost for the six-week pilot: $2,400, including recording and staff time.
Last month 18 visitors asked for audio descriptions; the museum does not know
how many other visitors would use them. The visitor-services team calls demand
"high" and says the recordings would be "a significant accessibility improvement."
No visitor trial has been run. Existing printed labels remain available.
A different proposed purchase, tablets for ticket scanning, costs $3,100.
Ticket staff say scanning is slow, but have not timed it.
The director has $2,500 available for this pilot and can approve only one.
Do not recommend that visitors bring a smartphone: many school groups cannot.
If approved, visitor-services staff would offer the players at the entrance,
record how many are borrowed, and collect optional feedback after each visit.
The director would review borrowing and feedback after six weeks before deciding
whether to add recordings for more paintings. There is no usage target yet.

### Quality bar

Evaluation mode: judgment.
Preserve meaning, uncertainty, and the reader's ability to act.

### Expectations

- Recommend an affordable pilot using the budget and observed requests.
- Preserve the scope, players, printed labels, optional feedback, and review.
- Distinguish measured demand from attributed opinions and unknown usefulness.
- Preserve who has not measured scanning time; do not broaden the claim.
- Openly labeled new advice is allowed; invented facts or existing rules fail.
- Do not invent a usage target or rely on smartphone access.

## C: Handbook revision

### Prompt

Use the writing guidance at `{GUIDANCE_PATH}`.
Use only the supplied facts.
Return the requested prose; do not change files.

Revise this handbook excerpt for staff who may land directly on any section
from a search result. Keep the section headings and all operational rules.
Do not add facts. Return the revised excerpt.

### Loan records

The Lantern desk app stores equipment loans. A borrower signs for each item
when the item leaves the building. Volunteers enter the borrower's name,
the item number, and the due date. The app keeps the signed form with the loan.

### Repairs

Equipment that needs repair goes on the orange rack. Volunteers attach a tag
describing the problem. The repair lead assigns each item to a technician.
Borrowers cannot reserve an item while it is on the orange rack.

### Donations

Donated equipment stays in the back room until the repair lead inspects it.
If the equipment passes inspection, a volunteer adds an inventory number and
places it in the lending cabinet. The donor can request a receipt at the desk.

### End of shift

Export the records from it before closing. Put the file in the shared folder.
The operations coordinator uses the file to find overdue items the next morning.
The shared folder is called `Daily loans`. Keep the paper forms in the locked
desk drawer for 30 days. This is an important part of maintaining a robust
accountability framework and ensuring successful follow-through.

### Winter hours

From November through February, the desk closes at 5 p.m. on weekdays.
Saturday hours stay 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. The repair team works after closing on
Tuesdays. Volunteers must leave the side entrance clear for the repair team.

### Corrections

If a borrower spots a mistake in the record, send it to the operations
coordinator. They correct it in the app and add a note explaining the correction.
Do not erase the signed form. This ensures that everyone remains aligned.

### Quality bar

Evaluation mode: judgment.
Preserve meaning, uncertainty, and the reader's ability to act.

### Expectations

- Make the Lantern app and loan records identifiable in each relevant section.
- Preserve all operational rules and section headings.
- Remove generic assurance and resolve distant pronouns using supported context.
- An ordinary grammatical reading of "send it" as sending the record is allowed.
- Do not invent a new form or procedure to resolve an ambiguity.

## D: Opening a makerspace

### Prompt

Use the writing guidance at `{GUIDANCE_PATH}`.
Use only the supplied facts.
Return the requested prose; do not change files.

Please improve this note from me, Jo, to the volunteers opening our makerspace.
It will go in tomorrow's volunteer email.

Good news: we've found the missing cupboard key. It was in the mug marked
"KEYS", which is a humbling development for a room full of engineers.
Thanks, Bea, for looking even after the rest of us gave up.

For Saturday, the welcome desk is not just an arrival point, but an essential
gateway to a meaningful and inclusive making experience. Our process is built
around three pillars: making people welcome, helping them feel at home, and
creating a friendly welcome. Ask visitors to sign in at the desk and show them
where to leave coats. Parents must stay with children under 12.

We have seen substantial interest in the soldering session. Five people asked
about it at last week's open evening. We have not opened bookings yet, and
we do not know how many of the five will come. Bea will demonstrate soldering
at 11 a.m. There are six places at the bench. To be clear, these are not six
guaranteed attendees, but six available places.

The workshop room has high noise levels. During the table-saw demonstration,
two visitors told us they could not hear the welcome talk next door. We have
not measured sound levels. We will move the welcome talk to the kitchen this
Saturday and ask visitors afterward whether they could hear it.

We are not requiring volunteers to stay after 2 p.m. Pack away anything you
use before you leave. I'll deal with the mountain of mugs. If you happen to
find my tea under it, please send a search party.

Overall, this represents an important step toward a robust and welcoming
environment, underscoring the continued value of our collective efforts.

### Quality bar

Evaluation mode: judgment.
Preserve meaning, uncertainty, and the reader's ability to act.

### Expectations

- Keep the sender's useful humor and gratitude while removing generic praise.
- Replace vague demand and noise measures with the supplied observations.
- Preserve unknown attendance, unmeasured sound levels, and the kitchen trial.
- Retain sign-in, coats, parent supervision, demonstration time and capacity,
  packing away, and the absence of a requirement to stay after 2 p.m.
- Do not turn that absence of obligation into a prohibition on staying.

### Quality bar
